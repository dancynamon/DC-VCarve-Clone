Matched piece-for-piece by bounding box, ignoring program order, all 21 passes agree:

  worst piece-size difference    0.0000"

So every piece is cut identically - same path, same 8 arcs, same direction, same entry rule.
Only the sequence differs, and because entry:'nearest' chains each entry to where the last cut
finished, a different sequence moves every entry point with it. That is why the harness reports
this as a cut-order failure on pass 2 rather than 20 separate ones.

  (3,3) (4,3) (4,2) (4,1) (4,0)                      [(col,row) on the 5x4 grid]

It is none of the things we can compute from the file:

  - not DXF order, which is plain row-major: (0,0) (1,0) (2,0) ... (4,3)
  - not nearest-neighbour. At step 3 the tool is at (4.17, 8.48); piece (1,1)'s nearest point
    is 5.17" away and piece (0,2)'s is 7.18". NN takes (1,1); the reference takes (0,2).
  - not a travel optimiser either. Total rapid travel between passes: reference 153.22",
    ours 124.83". Our nearest-neighbour tour is 19% SHORTER than Vectric's.
  - not recoverable from layers or handles: all 20 pieces sit on one layer, MALE, with no
    per-piece attributes, listed row-major.

The order almost certainly comes from the Aspire project - the sequence the pieces were nested
or created in - which the DXF export flattens away. source.crv3d is unreadable binary. Short of
reading the .crv3d, there is nothing in our inputs that could produce this sequence, and no
rule worth inventing from it.
